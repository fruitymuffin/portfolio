A commitment to ethical conduct and the highest standards of professional accountability
A rigorous set of safety rules and requirements are in place for the competition that UTS Motorsports Electric participates in. One of these dictates the section and thickness requirements of the roll cage of the car. When the car construction was almost complete, a chance inspection found that the steel tube thickness we were using was too small. Although the cars are inspected as part of the competition, this error could easily be overlooked and probably would not be flagged. As part of the team leadership, I had to help decide how to proceed, given that rectifying the mistake would be costly and time consuming. Of course, the tube was replaced, which was uncomfortable because it meant that the budget for some other areas had to be reduced. The purpose of the rules is to ensure the design is as safe as possible, and this issue prompted our decision to recheck all rules and ensure factors of safety were present on all suspension components.

Demonstrated ability to use and manage information
At UTS Rapido my first task was the development of a dissolved oxygen sensor. Rather than just including calculations in an excel sheet, I tried to create documentation which had a logical flow and was clearly described. Each section included the goal of the module, relevant information sources and calculations. Finally, a summary of how the calculations affected design decisions was included for each section. This style of documentation ensured that handovers to other engineers was smooth easy and was well received by coworkers.
